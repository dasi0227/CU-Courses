#### What kind of person would you want to be in that world?

**Knowing exactly what I want to achieve at each stage of my life** and **Having the ability to actually achieve it**.

These two things go hand in hand. The more capable you become, the higher your goals will be. And the higher your goals are, the more they push you to improve yourself.

But I think many people including myself today are stuck in a trap. They don't really know how capable they are. On one hand, they worry that setting their goals too low means missing out on better opportunities. On the other hand, they want to aim high, but that also puts a lot of pressure on them.

I think life is basically a constant cycle of finding goals and trying to achieve them. Finding a goal gives you freedom in your mind, while achieving it gives you freedom in the real world.

#### Which of your current ambitions would still matter?

**1. Always keep learning.**

Computer science is probably one of the fastest-changing fields in the world, both in terms of speed and scale. If I stop learning, I could fall behind in just one year. (maybe one month in 2027 😭)

**2. Always stay resilient.**

Whenever people try to improve themselves, they usually have to go through some really dark and difficult times. I believe these experiences are what shape a person's character. Only those who manage to get through them have a chance to succeed, while those who give up often end up failing. Resilience can not determine how high you can aim, but it determines whether you can actually reach the goals you set for yourself.


#### What would you want to be known for beyond your productivity and professional achievements?

**Sense of aesthetics**. 

I think aesthetics is something abstract, but other people can notice it almost immediately. Many people may not understand how technically difficult a product is to build, but they can still tell a lot about its quality just from the user experience and visual design. Sometimes, these things alone are enough to make people willing to pay for a product.

I also believe that a person's sense of aesthetics depends on how deeply they understand different things. To some extent, it even reflects their values and how they see the world.

#### What are you willing to struggle for, even when an AI agent could make the struggle unnecessary?

**Learning how to build things**.

Nowadays, AI makes building something incredibly easy. Even people with no computer science background can vibe-code an entire product. So I think the focus is no longer just on what you build, but on how you build it. Being able to explain your design rationale and demonstrate that you understand each step is what truly separates different ability levels.

#### What skills, qualities, or experiences would still make you valuable?

**Product Sense**.

I come up with a lot of ideas every day, whether it's building a website, creating a tool, or designing a physical product. But I don't just come up with an idea and leave it there. I like thinking about what the product is supposed to achieve, and why it should exist in the first place. I also try to connect these ideas with the latest market trends to see whether they actually make sense.

#### If your value is no longer determined primarily by what you can do, how will you define who you are?

**The emotional value I can bring to other people**.

AI mainly expresses emotions through text, but human emotions are much more than that. They also come from facial expressions, gestures, physical touch, and knowing what to say in different situations. (maybe at that point, we will go back to a world where everyone is basically a salesperson 😂)
